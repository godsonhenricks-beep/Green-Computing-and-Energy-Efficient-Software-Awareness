# Green Computing Quiz (10 questions)

1. Which has lower time complexity for finding duplicates: nested loops or a set-based approach?
2. True/False: Faster code always uses less energy overall.
3. Name two ways to reduce energy use of an idle server.
4. What does "greenwashing" mean?
5. Why should you profile code before optimizing?
6. Which is usually more energy-efficient for large files: loading all data at once or streaming it?
7. Give one reason caching can reduce energy use.
8. Why is estimated energy (runtime × power) not the same as measured energy?
9. Name two reliable sources for energy statistics on data centres.
10. Why should AI-generated statistics be verified?

---
## Answer Key
1. The set-based approach (O(n) vs O(n²)).
2. False — hardware, parallelism and idle power also matter; it often helps but not always.
3. Shut down or power-manage idle machines; consolidate via virtualization; right-size resources.
4. Making misleading or unsupported claims that a product or practice is environmentally friendly.
5. To find real bottlenecks so effort goes where it saves the most.
6. Streaming (lower memory and often less work).
7. It avoids recomputing the same results.
8. It ignores other components, idle power and variation in real power draw.
9. e.g., IEA, Green Software Foundation, peer-reviewed papers, government reports.
10. AI can hallucinate or cite outdated/non-existent data.
